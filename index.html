Protein per 100 kcal is the column worth studying: it says how much protein a food carries for the calories it costs. Egg white, white fish and fat-free dairy sit at the top; nuts and cheese look strong per 100 g but drop sharply here, because most of their weight is fat.
Portion figures use the first household measure for each food — the amounts are shown so you can see what is being assumed.

119 of the 130 built-in foods take their protein figure straight from USDA Standard Reference 28, the dataset FoodData Central serves as “SR Legacy”; each row carries its NDB id in a comment so it can be re-checked. The 11 marked ≈ have no USDA entry and come from product labels — check your own tub, especially for protein powders.
Raw vs. cooked matters: cooked meat lost water, so it is denser in protein. Weigh your food the same way the row is labelled.
Foods can be searched in English, German or Polish, with or without accents — kase finds Käse, losos finds łosoś. The German and Polish names are hand-written rather than taken from a database; they are listed in translations.tsv and corrections there are welcome.
